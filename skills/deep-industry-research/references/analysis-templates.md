## 1. Technology Route Selection

**Decision question:** Which route is likely to win in each application, and why?

| Route | Customer problem solved | Selection metric | Current performance | Cost | Manufacturability | Maturity | Customer adoption | Substitute risk | Investment implication |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|

Separate theoretical performance from production yield, reliability, qualification, and total system cost.

## 2. Commercialization Ladder

| Application/customer group | Current stage | Concrete evidence | Buyer and budget | Qualification cycle | Next milestone | Failure mode | Confidence |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|

Use the stages: lab, prototype, pilot, customer validation, design-in, design-win/nomination, mass production, recognized revenue, cash collection.

## 3. Industry Stage and Inflection Timeline

**Decision question:** Where are we on the adoption curve, and what opens or closes the investment window?

| Time/stage | Technology state | Customer behavior | Supply structure | Cost/economic state | Trigger event | Verification signal | Investment implication |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|

Do not infer industry stage from CR5 alone. Combine demand validation, standards, qualification, production scale, cost curve, supply-chain lock-in, and concentration.

For each core driver (technology, policy, demand), apply the driver-disappearance test: if that driver vanished, would the industry still exist? Record the result in the table's `Trigger event` or `Verification signal` column — a sector that fails the test carries structural demand risk regardless of current growth.

## 14. Table and Page Quality Rules

### Complete analysis unit

Build each core page or section around:

1. a conclusion-style title;
2. 3–5 evidence points;
3. one useful table, chart, timeline, or diagram;
4. causal reasoning;
5. investment implication;
6. bear point;
7. confidence and validation need;
8. trigger-company implication when applicable.

### Table rules

- Use a table for comparison, chronology, calculation, ranking, or contradiction.
- Prefer 4–10 rows and 4–10 decision-useful columns.
- Use concrete mechanisms, metrics, milestones, and evidence; avoid matrices made only of `高/中/低`.
- Add a conclusion below every decisive table.
- Cite sources in cells, captions, or adjacent notes.
- Split a table when it will become illegible in Word or PDF.
- Use prose when nuance matters more than alignment.

### Density rules

- Do not complete a core chapter with one or two overview paragraphs.
- Do not increase word count without adding evidence, comparison, mechanism, or investment implication.
- If evidence is thin, show the gap, the implication of uncertainty, and the next research action.
