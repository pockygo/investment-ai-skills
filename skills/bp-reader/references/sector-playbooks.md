# Sector Playbooks

Use only the section relevant to the company being screened. These notes adjust the general BP screening framework; they do not replace evidence discipline.

## AI / Software / Agent

Focus on:
- Workflow urgency and budget owner, not just model capability.
- Distribution advantage, data access, integration depth, and switching cost.
- Gross margin after inference, human-in-the-loop delivery, implementation, and support.
- Retention, expansion, usage frequency, and whether the product is system-of-record or nice-to-have.
- Dependency on foundation model providers, open-source substitution, and price compression.

Key red flags:
- Demo impressive but no repeatable paid use case.
- Revenue mostly custom projects or consulting.
- Claims proprietary model advantage without benchmark, data rights, or deployment proof.
- CAC, implementation effort, or inference cost destroys SaaS-like economics.

## Enterprise Software / SaaS

Focus on:
- ARR/MRR, net revenue retention, gross retention, logo retention, ACV, sales cycle, payback period.
- ICP clarity and whether buyer, user, and budget owner are aligned.
- Implementation cost, customer success burden, channel reliance, and churn drivers.
- Whether growth is founder-led sales or repeatable GTM.

Key red flags:
- Signed customers but no recurring revenue.
- High ACV supported by one-off customization.
- Weak renewal data despite claiming PMF.
- Channel partners own customer relationship.

## Semiconductor / Hard Tech / Advanced Manufacturing

Focus on:
- Technical route, process maturity, yield, reliability, certification, and customer validation stage.
- Position in the supply chain and whether the company solves a bottleneck.
- Domestic substitution logic: who currently supplies, why replacement is possible, and switching barriers.
- Capex intensity, manufacturing scalability, BOM, gross margin, and delivery timeline.
- Pilot, sample, design-in, design-win, mass production, and paid order distinctions.
- Commercialization ladder: `sample/prototype -> pilot -> design-in -> design-win/nomination -> mass production -> recognized revenue -> cash collection`. Force the memo to place each major customer and product on this ladder when the BP is ambiguous.

Key red flags:
- Equates pilot or sample delivery with mass-production validation.
- Uses customer logos or "coverage" without disclosing order status, revenue contribution, or collection.
- Patent count without process know-how or customer qualification.
- Long validation cycles unsupported by runway.
- Customer concentration or dependence on one strategic partner.

## Biotech / Medtech

Focus on:
- Indication, target, mechanism, clinical stage, endpoint, patient population, and standard of care.
- Regulatory path, trial design, safety profile, and comparable clinical data.
- IP ownership, freedom to operate, licensing obligations, and CMC/manufacturing.
- Reimbursement, hospital adoption, physician workflow, and commercialization partner.

Key red flags:
- Preclinical data presented as clinical validation.
- Unclear regulatory classification or approval pathway.
- Small or crowded indication without differentiation.
- Pipeline depends on expensive trials beyond available financing.

## New Energy / Climate / Materials

Focus on:
- Cost curve, energy density, efficiency, degradation, safety, cycle life, supply chain, and scale-up.
- Customer qualification stage and bankability.
- Unit economics under realistic commodity prices and subsidy assumptions.
- Manufacturing yield, capex, working capital, and project delivery risk.

Key red flags:
- Economics depend mainly on subsidy or optimistic commodity prices.
- Lab result not proven at pilot or commercial scale.
- Capex needs exceed plausible financing plan.
- Claims ESG demand without procurement willingness.

## Consumer / Brand / Retail

Focus on:
- Repeat purchase, cohort retention, gross margin, channel mix, CAC, inventory turnover, and working capital.
- Brand differentiation, SKU productivity, price band, category frequency, and distribution quality.
- Organic traffic versus paid growth.
- Whether early GMV is sustainable or promotion-driven.

Key red flags:
- GMV growth but weak gross margin or high return/refund rate.
- Dependence on one platform or influencer channel.
- Inventory and receivables consume cash faster than revenue scales.
- Brand story stronger than repurchase evidence.

## Healthcare Services

Focus on:
- Payer, reimbursement, utilization frequency, physician/resource constraint, compliance, and local expansion model.
- Unit economics by site, physician, bed, visit, or procedure.
- Standardization and replicability across cities or hospitals.

Key red flags:
- Revenue growth driven by opening sites with poor same-store economics.
- Regulatory or license exposure not addressed.
- Doctor resources are not scalable.

## Education

Focus on:
- Regulatory constraints, learning outcome proof, renewal, completion, refund, and channel cost.
- B2B/B2G sales cycle versus B2C retention.
- Content/product differentiation and whether AI lowers production cost or increases competition.

Key red flags:
- Policy sensitivity ignored.
- LTV/CAC unclear or depends on aggressive paid acquisition.
- Outcome claims lack measurable evidence.
